## Stress-test every venture and project (premortem protocol)

Standing instruction from the founder (2026-10-03): apply this to any proposed venture, project, offer, feature or major spend before recommending or committing to it. Do not ask "is this a good plan?"; assume it has already failed six months from now and work backwards, so problems surface while they are still cheap to fix.

1. **Failure autopsy.** The seven most likely reasons it failed, most likely first. For each: what caused it, how it developed, the assumption that was wrong, the early warning sign that was ignored, and the detail of this plan that supports it. No generic advice.
2. **Fatal assumption.** The most likely failure and the most damaging one, and why they differ. The single biggest assumption being treated as fact, the evidence that would prove it wrong before more time or money goes in, and any flaw that makes the plan unsound, stated plainly.
3. **Rebuild the plan.** Keep the objective; change strategy, sequence, timeline, budget, dependencies or execution where needed, naming the risk each change reduces. Then five pre-launch checks, each with a GO, ADJUST and ABANDON result.
4. **Think like the competitor.** How the smartest rival who benefits from failure would attack before launch, during launch week and right after; the weakness least likely to be noticed; defensive moves to make now.
5. **Tripwires.** One measurable signal per major failure mode, with thresholds and a check cadence (weekly by default): GREEN continue normally, YELLOW investigate and adjust, RED stop, rethink or change direction.

Say clearly when a venture or project looks counterproductive. The full protocol, tools and standard tripwires live in the private tme-kb repository (`docs/08-stress-test-protocol.md`, Biz Dev Hub Premortem and Tripwire Tracker).
